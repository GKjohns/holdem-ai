What was built

A local Nuxt 4 app where you sit at a no-limit Texas Hold'em cash game (2–6 seats, full NLHE rules: min-raise, all-ins, side and split pots, button rotation, eliminations) against AI opponents, each seat a different model. In Play mode you pick 1–5 opponents and play hands; each model's reasoning or probability spread stays sealed until the hand ends, then opens up in a per-seat review, and a session leaderboard appears when you leave or bust. In Watch mode (pulled forward from bonus B1) 2–6 AIs play each other unattended with every card and every thought shown live. Two kinds of decider sit behind one shared action menu: general LLMs (GPT-6, Claude 5.5, Gemini 3.1) called through generateText + Output.object, and the decision models gpt-6-luna-decisions and Jev called through experimental_evaluate (an alias of experimental_decide in ai@7), which return a choice plus probabilities instead of prose. Kyle's rule holds throughout: an AI gets only what a human at the table has. The only model input is seatView(), which carries the seat's own cards, the board, chip counts, positions and the action log. It has no odds, equity or hand-strength helpers.

Chip-conservation property test

500 seeded hands of random legal actions across 106 tables. After every hand the sum of stacks equals the starting chips. The walk covered 330 showdowns, 117 multi-pot hands, 28 spots where a short all-in did not reopen action, and 14 split pots. npm run typecheck and npm run lint are clean, and / returns 200 with the header and color-mode toggle.

Rule decisions

  • Callers owe the full big blind even when the BB is short (currentBet = bigBlind).
  • Re-raise eligibility for a seat that already acted is currentBet − committed ≥ lastRaiseSize. A single short all-in doesn't reopen action. Several short all-ins that add up to a full raise do, which is the TDA cumulative rule.
  • canRaise is false when no other live seat has chips behind. A lone seat that covers every all-in gets no decision, and the board runs out.
  • Uncalled chips are returned at the end of each betting round, so the pot is right during all-in runouts.
  • Odd chips go one at a time, starting left of the button. The button moves (no dead button), and the first hand's button is the human.
  • The deck for each hand is seeded with seed ^ handNo·φ, so any hand replays from (seed, handNo). The engine burns a card before each street.
  • Menu wording uses chips and the resulting pot only, e.g. Raise to 250, raising by the size of the pot (pot becomes 400). It never states odds or a judgment.

Deviations and known issues

  • Extra exports (TableConfig, LegalActions, potTotal, positionOf, withTableTalk, compareHands…) beyond the contract.
  • No resting showdown street: the engine goes straight to complete with result set, and the UI stages the reveal.
  • Blinds are not ActionEvents. They show up as lastAction and in committed.
  • Busted seats are also folded: true, and they're left out of SeatView.opponents and deltas.

Visual proof: none. This sprint is engine-only and is proven by tests. Dark mode was deferred to Sprint 3's screenshots.

Pilot gate

First 5-hand pilot failed the gate. Luna Decisions refused one flop spot (top set facing a check) with DecisionRefusalError on 4 of 4 tries. That made its fallback rate 6.3%, over the 5% bar (selfplay-pilot-5-failed.txt). The fix was to have decide() retry a refusal once with "Which option is the best poker play here?". The re-pilot had 0 fallbacks across all three models (selfplay-pilot-5.txt), and only then did the 20-hand run go ahead.

The cost estimator was off by the gate's own measure: it predicted $0.00025/hand and the pilot measured $0.00065 (2.6×). Random dry-run play folds hands early, and decision-model JSON costs more tokens than chars/4 counts. The estimator was recalibrated; the gate was not loosened. The new estimate is $0.0006/hand, and the 20-hand run measured $0.00062/hand.

Deviations and known issues

  • experimental_evaluate is a deprecated alias of experimental_decide in ai@7. They are the same function.
  • Luna Decisions effort: providerOptions.openai.reasoningEffort comes back as an unsupported warning with the same answer and latency, so it stays at default.
  • Haiku 5.5 set to reasoning: 'low'. Unset averaged 2.5 s with a 4.5 s / 497-token tail; low averaged 1.6 s and made the same choice on 4 of 5 views (haiku-latency.txt).
  • Prompts call the human seat "Human player", and preflop blinds are derived from committed − logged chips because blinds aren't log events.
  • The harness rebuys busted seats and checks conservation against starting chips plus rebuys. --out does not save views.
  • Gap handed to Sprint 4: SeatView had no seat order or opponent positions (fixed in 4.4b). Refusal retries were only logged (also fixed in 4.4b).

End-to-end golden path

One session from setup to summary, using the screenshots above in order. The Hands and Chips shots come from a Watch session, because those tabs fill fastest there. The same components render in Play mode, which shows only showdown cards and your own.

  1. Setup: pick opponents, stack, blinds; Deal me in.
  2. Your turn: blinds posted, AIs act through /api/decide, action reaches you.
  3. Showdown: cards up, best five highlighted, review unlocks.
  4. Hands tab: the finished hand is in the history with its full log.
  5. Chips tab: stacks over hands.
  6. Summary: Leave table → leaderboard with cost.

Step 0: the 901 s decision

No code path was missing the abort signal: both SDK paths pass it (decide() → retry → doDecide; generateText → fetch). pmset -g log shows a clamshell sleep at 00:57:42 EDT, a 900 s maintenance sleep, and a DarkWake at 01:13:33. The fold landed at 05:13:36Z, 3 s after wake. Timers run on the monotonic clock, which stops while the Mac sleeps, so the 30 s timer had barely run. Fixes anyway: bounded() races one hard deadline over the whole attempt (SDK retry and refusal re-ask included), so a path that ignores the signal still returns. A timeout whose wall time exceeds its awake time by > 5 s is asked once more. The runner adds a 45 s wall-clock watchdog per decision and 5 min per hand, and batches run under caffeinate -ims. tests/decide.test.ts covers it with mock models that never settle and ignore the signal: LLM hang, error then hang through the SDK retry, refusal then hang on the reworded retry, and caller abort, all bounded at the deadline.
